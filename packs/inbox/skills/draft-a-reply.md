---
name: "Draft a reply"
description: "Writes a reply to one email in the person's voice, ready for them to check and send."
owner: "Drafter"
---
Goal
Write a reply the person can send with little or no editing.

When to use
- The person asks for a reply to an email ("draft a reply to Sam", "say no politely").
- InboxManager hands over an email that needs an answer.

Inputs
- email: the email to answer (from the connected account, or pasted by the person).
- intent: what the reply should do: yes, no, ask a question, set a time, say thanks. If it's unclear, ask one short question first.
- tone: friendly by default. Use what the person has said about how they write.

Steps
1. Read the whole thread, not just the last message. Note the question being asked and any deadline.
2. Write the reply:
   - A greeting that matches how the sender wrote (first name if they used one).
   - The answer in the first sentence.
   - At most 3 short paragraphs.
   - One clear next step or question.
   - The person's usual sign-off, or "Best," and their first name.
3. Mark every fact you weren't given with [check], such as a date, price, address or attachment.
4. If the tone is unclear, give two versions with a one-line label each (for example "firm" and "softer").
5. Post the draft in the chat. If Gmail or Outlook is connected, also save it there as a draft and say so.

Checks
- The reply answers the sender's actual question.
- It promises nothing the person didn't say: no dates, prices, refunds or commitments of your own.
- It's shorter than the email it answers, unless the person asked for more.
- No [check] mark is left unexplained.

What can go wrong
- The thread is long or in another language: summarise it in 2 lines first, then draft in the sender's language.
- The email is a complaint: acknowledge the problem first, then the fix or next step. No blame.
- The email asks for money, codes or sign-in details: don't draft a reply that gives them. Tell the person it may be phishing.

Never
- Send the email. Saving a draft is the most you do.
- Add attachments, CCs or BCCs the person didn't ask for.
