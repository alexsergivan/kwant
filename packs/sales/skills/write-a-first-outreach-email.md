---
name: "Write a first outreach email"
description: "Drafts a short, personal first email to one lead, with two subject lines, ready for the person to send."
owner: "OutreachWriter"
---
Goal
A first email a busy person would actually read and answer.

When to use
- The person or SalesLead picks a lead and asks for an email.
- After the leads list, when the person says "write to the top 3".

Inputs
- lead: company, why it fits, who to contact (from Prospector's list).
- offer: what the person sells and the main benefit, in their words.
- sender: the person's name and role. Ask if you don't know.

Steps
1. Find one specific, true reason to write to this company now, from the lead notes (for example, a new shop opening, a job post, a product they launched).
2. Write the email:
   - Subject: two options, under 6 words each, no clickbait.
   - Line 1: the specific reason, about them, not you.
   - Lines 2 to 3: the problem you solve for companies like them, in their terms, with one concrete result if the person has given one.
   - Last line: one easy question, such as "Worth a 15-minute call next week?"
   - Sign-off with the sender's name.
3. Keep it under 120 words. No attachments, no links unless the person gave one.
4. Write a follow-up for 4 days later: 2 to 3 lines that add one new thing, not "just bumping this".
5. Post both in the chat. If a mail app is connected and the person asks, save them as drafts.

Checks
- Under 120 words, one question, no buzzwords ("synergy", "revolutionary", "game-changer").
- Every fact about the company is in the lead notes.
- No price, discount, deadline or promise the person didn't give.

What can go wrong
- No specific reason to write: say so and suggest skipping the lead or finding one first.
- The contact is a person's private address: don't use it. Use the company's contact route.

Never
- Send the email or schedule it.
- Write to more than one lead in one email (no BCC lists).
