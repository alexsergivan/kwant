New in 0.4.0 · Teams

Work handed on, checked and seen

Teammates pass work along on their own, the bot that handed it out checks the result, and a bot can look at a page instead of only reading it.

  • The lead reviews hand-offs

    When a teammate finishes, the bot that handed out the work reviews it: a pass makes the card Verified, a fail sends it back with the reason. It gets one reminder, then the card says "Waiting for <lead> to review". A bot still can't approve its own card.

  • Notices don't wait in Review

    A one-way hand-off nobody is asked about is shown as Closed, "Notice, no review needed", never as Verified.

  • @mentions in a result hand work on

    When QA's result says "@Frontend could switch the header", Frontend gets that task even if the lead doesn't pass it on. The lead hears back once, with every result.

  • One @mention picks who answers

    "@Frontend why didn't you answer QA" goes to Frontend, even though it names QA. Two @mentions still go to the lead.

  • Bots see the page

    A browser_screenshot tool hands Claude and Codex bots a real picture of the page, at a width they choose (390 for a phone, 1280 for a desktop), so a designer can check layout, colours and spacing.

  • No token limits by default

    Tasks, missions and hand-offs no longer stop at a hidden token budget. Set one in Settings → Usage if you want it. Hand-off caps (4 per bot, 16 per request) still stop loops, with a Continue button.

New in 0.4.0 · Performance & audits

Learn from the work that's already done

See which bot and model does each kind of work well, have a second bot challenge important work, and turn work done well into skills. All of it needs the Mission board, and the Auditor and Skill Foundry are off by default.

Who does each kind of work well

Verified rates by bot or by model over the last 30 days, 90 days or all time. Report-only: it never decides who gets work.

Open risks on the task

When the Auditor is on, a second bot's risks sit on the task with their evidence and the owner's answer. You resolve or dismiss each one.

  • Performance (report-only)

    Under the board, per kind of work and bot or model: tasks finished, the verified rate with its likely range, median time and tokens, cost per task and how often you stepped in. Groups with fewer than 5 tasks say "few tasks". It never changes who gets work.

  • Swarm Auditor (off by default)

    An important task, or one no outside check will judge, gets one challenge when it reaches review: a teammate from outside the lead's loop lists risks with evidence, and the owner answers once. An audit never verifies, fails or moves a task. Up to 5 a day by default, on a fast, cheap model unless you pick another.

  • Open risks

    What an audit leaves unresolved is listed on the task, with its evidence and the owner's answer, until you Resolve or Dismiss it. Board cards count the open risks.

  • Skill Foundry (off by default)

    When a bot has 3 or more verified tasks of one kind of work in 60 days, 70% of them verified, it drafts a skill from them. Each draft is replayed as a dry run on up to 3 of those tasks in a scratch copy, and waits in the Skills tab for you to approve, edit or reject. Versions can be rolled back. It's switched on through the API for now.

Mission board

Work your bots can prove they finished

One board for every task your bots take on. A task counts as Verified only when a check passes, never on a bot's word.

One board, five columns

Missions, hand-offs, routine runs and loose ends you take on, from Ready to Verified. Needs you collects every question, approval and failure waiting on you.

Verified outcomes, with evidence

A check proves it: a command that passes, a page that answers, a file that holds the right text, a teammate's review or your OK. Each task keeps the receipt.

Checkpoints and restore

kwant snapshots a task's folder before and after every run. Preview a checkpoint and restore it: only that task's files go back, and you can undo the restore.

AutoFix

A failed task says why and what to fix. AutoFix retries what is safe to retry, within a budget, and asks first if you prefer. Sign-in problems and denied actions always come back to you.

Fails, retries, Verified

A load-test report misses its p95 latency, so the check fails. AutoFix runs it again with what the check said, and the task ends Verified.

How AutoFix works →

  • Missions

    Group tasks toward one goal, with a token limit if you want one. Tasks wait for the ones they build on.

  • No marking its own homework

    A check a bot adds to its own task waits for your OK before it counts.

  • Change approvals

    A bot can propose changing a check or the acceptance. You see before and after, and nothing changes until you approve.

  • "May be invalid"

    When a file a verified task relied on changes, the task and those built on it are flagged for you to keep or redo.

  • Insights

    Tasks verified this week, false successes, what needs you now, and what AutoFix fixed.

  • On a phone

    One column at a time, with tabs that count the tasks in each.

Flight School

Freedom a bot earns, one kind of work at a time

Give each bot a tier per kind of work: Observe, Sandbox or Act. A tier only adds approval cards, and payments always ask.

A Sandbox bot asks first

QA works at Sandbox on releases. It may change files in its task folder, but a build command asks, and the card says why. You allow it once, and the task ends Verified.

What Sandbox asks about →

Tiers per bot and per kind of work

Sandbox for everything, Observe for deploys, Act for release notes. Revoke lowers a tier in one click, from the bot's next action.

Off until you want it

Switch it on in Settings. Bots you have stay at Act, new bots start at Sandbox, and nothing changes on screen while it's off.

Payments always ask

Anything that looks like paying shows a card, at every tier and even with approvals off. It says why, and has no Always button.

Risks said before approvals go off

Choosing "Don't ask" first says plainly what bots can then do on their own. Nothing changes until you press Turn off approvals.

  • Observe

    Plans, reads and reports. Every change asks you first.

  • Sandbox

    Changes files in its task folder when they can be undone. Commands that can change things ask.

  • Act

    Works as it does today, with its usual approvals.

  • Engines it can't watch

    A bot on Codex or a Custom API account below Act doesn't run its task. It waits in Needs you and says what to do.

Connect apps

Bots find a way round

Before a bot says "not possible", it tries another route, and when it needs an app you haven't connected, it hands you a card.

Connect an app from a bot's card

Asked about search clicks, TechLead reads the site itself, then offers Google Search Console. Press Connect, sign in on the app's own page, and the card says Connected.

MCP tools & Composio →

Reads what a page hides

A new page_source tool reads a public page's tags: description, canonical, robots, Open Graph, headings, alt text and structured data.

A card, not a dead end

Without access to an app, a bot offers a connection card and carries on with what it can do. Without a ready Composio connector, the card says what's missing.

Teams & chat

A team of bots that talk to each other

Each bot has a role, a voice and a model, and knows who else is in the room.

Teams with a lead

Give a team a mission, pinned instructions every member follows, a lead and a shared workspace.

Personas and souls

Start from a preset. Every bot has a persona for what it does, a soul for how it speaks, and a pixel avatar.

  • Handoffs with an @mention

    Mention a teammate, or @everyone, and they answer next in the same chat. Caps of 4 per bot and 16 per message stop loops, with Continue.

  • Team discussions

    Ask a team and the lead runs a short discussion, then posts one summary. Teammates can pass when they have nothing to add.

  • Channels and direct chats

    Talk to the whole team in a channel or to one bot directly. Threads keep side topics apart.

  • The Laya router

    Picks which bot answers in a channel, and the model and effort for bots set to auto. If it is offline, kwant routes by keyword.

  • A receipt for every tool

    Replies list the tools a bot used and how each call ended.

  • Works on a phone

    The web app fits a phone screen, with an inbox tab for what needs you.

Spaces & accounts

Separate spaces, every account

Keep work, side projects and home apart, and use all the accounts you pay for.

Rules per space

Each space has its own approval rule and hiring rule.

Accounts per space

See every account at a glance: which is the default, which is in use, and which ran out and when it's back.

  • Spaces

    Each space has its own bots, teams, channels, routines, skills, tool servers and accounts.

  • Quick switching

    Switch from the rail or with Ctrl/⌘ + 1–9. Every space has its own address.

  • Archive, not delete

    Archiving a space pauses its routines and keeps everything. Restore it from Archived spaces.

  • Several accounts per engine

    Claude, Codex and Grok CLI logins, Anthropic, OpenAI and xAI keys, with a default and an order.

  • Failover

    When an account runs out of usage or is signed out, the turn moves to the next one, up to three tries, and the chat says so.

  • Your own server

    Add any server that speaks the OpenAI chat or Anthropic Messages API, such as OpenRouter or LM Studio.

  • Sign in from the app

    Sign in a Claude, Codex or Grok CLI account from kwant itself.

  • Keys stay hidden

    Keys are write-only. kwant shows whether one is set, never the key.

Works on its own

kwant keeps going when you close the tab

Scheduled work, check-ins, and a list of everything still open.

Routines

Once, daily, weekly, monthly, every few minutes or hours, or a cron rule. Run now, history and pause for each.

Loose ends

Finds questions, promises and replies that went quiet in your chats, email and GitHub.

  • Follow-ups

    A bot can set itself a one-off check-in, like looking at a deploy in an hour.

  • While you were away

    What is waiting on you, what finished and what failed or got stuck, with questions and approvals answered in place.

  • Letters

    Photograph a bill or notice. kwant reads the sender, the ask, the deadline and amounts, and reminds you before it is due.

  • Never sends

    Loose ends and letters draft the reply. You copy it or open it in your mail app.

  • Parallel Universe

    Load-test up to three ways to deploy in sealed containers on your server, next to dated price estimates. No cloud account and no spending.

  • Notifications

    Browser notifications, or an HTTPS webhook such as ntfy for your phone.

Safety & control

Autonomy you control

Bots get on with ordinary work. You choose which steps ask first, and every change can be undone.

Hiring you approve

Bots can propose a new teammate. Accept once, always, or deny. Each space sets its rule.

Saved passwords

Website logins in kwant's encrypted vault or your own Bitwarden. Turning approvals off requires Bitwarden.

  • Approval cards

    Allow once, Always allow or Deny, with the exact command or arguments.

  • Three approval modes

    Ask only when risky, the default for new spaces. Or ask before running commands, or don't ask. Per space, and per bot.

  • Changes with an Undo

    Bots create routines, channels and bots when asked, each with an Undo. Deletions ask first.

  • Things bots can't change

    Keys and secrets, sign-ins, approval rules and computer mode stay yours.

  • Sign in your phone with a QR code

    Settings → Signed-in devices shows a one-time QR code for a new phone or browser, and lists your signed-in browsers with Sign out.

  • Approve a new device

    A new browser can ask for access with a 10-minute code. Your signed-in browsers show the code, the browser and its IP, with Approve or Deny.

  • kwant's secrets are off limits

    In every mode, tool calls that name kwant's own secret files are refused.

  • Safe imports

    Imported bots arrive with no tools and no keys. Their skills wait for approval and their routines start paused.

Your bots and knowledge

Bots that learn your way of working

And you can read, change or switch off everything they learn.

Memory

Up to 100 short memories per bot, written by you or the bot, all editable.

Skills

Written procedures a bot proposes and you approve. Turn each on or off.

  • Search

    Ctrl K or ⌘K searches every channel and direct chat in the open space.

  • Bring your bots

    Import from Grok, ChatGPT, Claude or a file such as a Claude Code subagent or a SKILL.md.

  • Export

    Export a space or one bot as a file, without chat history, files, tools or keys.

  • Write your own skill

    Add a skill to a bot yourself, from its Skills tab.

Running it

One binary, on your laptop or a server

No cloud service to sign up for.

Local or VM

Bots browse and run commands on your computer, or in Docker containers kept away from your files.

Usage

For API-key and custom-API bots, a Usage tab breaks tokens down by purpose, cache and call.

  • One binary

    kwnt has the web app and an SQLite database built in. No Node.js at runtime.

  • Laptop or VPS

    Nothing to sign in to locally. On a server, open the link it prints once. Docker Compose and a systemd unit are included.

  • Any CLI or API

    The claude, codex or grok CLI, or Anthropic, OpenAI or xAI keys.

  • MCP tools

    Add any MCP server and pick which bots can use it. OAuth for servers that need a sign-in.

  • Composio, optional

    Under Connections, add Composio to reach Gmail, Slack, Notion, Linear and more. It's off until you switch it on, and those sign-ins live on Composio's servers, not in kwant's vault.

  • A shared browser

    Bots browse in kwant's own browser profile, or in a container in VM mode.

  • Your data, one folder

    Chats, bots and settings live in ~/.kwnt by default.
