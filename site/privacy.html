This website

Hosting

kwant.si is served by Cloudflare Pages. When you open a page, your browser sends Cloudflare the usual request data: your IP address, the page address, date and time, browser and operating system (user agent) and the referring page. Cloudflare needs this to deliver the page and protect the site from attacks, and keeps it in short-lived logs under its own privacy policy. Cloudflare can also receive network error reports from your browser when a page fails to load. We don't see these logs as personal data and we don't combine them with anything else.

The legal basis is our legitimate interest in running a secure, working website (Article 6(1)(f) GDPR). Cloudflare is a processor for us and may process data outside the EU; Cloudflare relies on the EU-U.S. Data Privacy Framework and standard contractual clauses for this.

No cookies, analytics or third-party content

The site sets no cookies, uses no analytics or tracking, and loads no fonts, scripts or images from other servers. Its Content-Security-Policy only allows files from kwant.si itself. There are no forms.

Links to other sites

Links to GitHub (the source code, issues and the Mac app download) take you to GitHub, which processes your data under the GitHub privacy statement.

The kwant software

You install kwant on your own computer or server and you are in charge of it. Your bots, chats, files, memory, accounts and passwords are stored there, in kwant's data folder (~/.kwnt by default). We don't operate any kwant service, and nothing you do in kwant reaches us. If you run kwant for other people, you are the controller for their data.

What kwant connects to on its own

Without any setup, kwant makes only these connections:

  • Routing model (Laya). On first start, kwant installs its local routing model: Python packages from PyPI and the PyTorch download server, a Python installer (uv, from Astral via GitHub) if your computer has no Python, and the model files (about 420 MB) from Hugging Face. These servers see your IP address. After that the model runs on your machine. Start kwant with --no-laya or set KWNT_LAYA_MANAGED=0 to skip it.
  • Fonts in the web app. kwant's web interface and the Mac app load the Inter and JetBrains Mono fonts from Google Fonts, so Google sees your IP address when you open it (Google Fonts privacy).

There is no update check, crash reporting or usage statistics.

What kwant connects to when you set it up

Everything else happens only after you add an account, a connection or a tool, and sends what you or your bots give it to that service, under that service's terms:

  • AI model providers you sign in to or add a key for, directly or through their command-line tools: Anthropic (Claude), OpenAI (ChatGPT, Codex), xAI (Grok), Google (Gemini), OpenRouter and any compatible server you enter. Your messages, files the bots read and tool results go to the provider you chose.
  • Installing tools with kwnt setup or the in-app setup: npm, Homebrew, the Claude, Codex and Grok installers, Playwright's browser download, and Docker if you ask for it.
  • MCP servers and connectors you add, such as Composio, Notion, Linear or GitHub. Bots can also search the public MCP Registry by name to suggest a server; nothing is installed without you.
  • Loose ends sources you turn on: your email server (IMAP, read-only) and the GitHub API.
  • Passwords: Bitwarden, if you connect a vault.
  • Phone notifications: ntfy or the server you set, if you turn them on.
  • Websites your bots visit with the bot browser or web search. Those sites see a normal browser visit from your computer or server.
